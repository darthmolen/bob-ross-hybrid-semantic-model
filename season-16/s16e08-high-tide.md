# Season 16, Episode 8 — "High Tide" (1988)

## 1. Composition

- Tropical seascape built on a **dry black gesso ground**, not a white one
- **Two palm trunks** rising from the lower left: a heavy curving trunk that carries the full frond canopy across the upper left, and a second, thinner trunk crossing behind it and running off the top-left edge
- A single large wave breaking through the centre-right, its crest translucent green where the light passes through the water
- Secondary foam and spray running along the left, breaking against the dark foreground rocks
- Glowing yellow sunset sky at the horizon, banded above by heavy mauve-violet storm cloud
- Dark rocky headland and low bushes anchoring the lower left corner
- No structures, no figures

A backlit tropical surf composition worked as light emerging from a black ground.

Composition archetype: **Tropical Seascape With Palms**

## 2. Palette

- **Black Gesso** as the ground itself — every colour above is laid over dry black, so value comes from how much light is added, not from how much dark is mixed in
- **Cadmium Yellow** and **Indian Yellow** carrying the glowing horizon; because they sit over black they are scumbled thin and read as light rather than pigment
- **Phthalo Blue** for the ocean body and the cool cloud shadow
- **Alizarin Crimson** and **Bright Red** mixed into the upper cloud mass for the mauve-violet storm tone
- **Titanium White** for foam, spray and the translucent wave crest — the only heavily loaded colour in the painting
- **Sap Green** for the palm fronds and the low foreground bushes
- **Van Dyke Brown** and **Dark Sienna** for the palm trunks and the foreground rock
- **Yellow Ochre** warming the sand and the lit edges of the rocks
- **Midnight Black** reinforcing the darkest foreground accents

There is no Prussian Blue and no Phthalo Green on this palette. The translucent green inside the wave is Cadmium Yellow lifted through the thin Phthalo Blue over black, not a green straight from the knife.

Palette identity: **golden backlit surf over a black ground**

## 3. Mood & Atmosphere

- Dramatic and energetic — the wave is caught at the moment of collapse
- Storm light: a bright, low sun burning under a heavy cloud lid
- Tropical warmth held in tension against turbulent water
- Luminosity that comes out of darkness rather than off a white base

Lighting: **backlit storm glow** with the sun low behind the breaking wave.

## 4. Structural Layout

- **Left:** Two palm trunks, dark rock headland, bushes, and the tail of the breaking foam
- **Right:** Open ocean and rolling swell running to the horizon
- **Centre:** The primary wave, its translucent crest the brightest passage in the painting
- **Foreground:** Dark rocks, spray and the churned water at the base of the break
- **Midground:** Building swells carrying the eye back to the horizon line
- **Background:** Yellow horizon glow beneath a mauve-violet cloud band

Depth style: **horizontal banded layers with silhouette framing**

## 5. Motion

- The wave breaking right-to-left with the crest already curling over
- Foam and spray thrown forward off the break
- Palm fronds bent by the onshore wind
- Cloud drift running across the top of the canvas against the wave direction

Motion profile: **breaking-wave collapse against a wind-driven sky**

## 6. Technique

- Black gesso ground applied and allowed to dry completely before any oil
- Sky and horizon glow scumbled thin over the dry black so the ground shows through and darkens the value naturally
- Ocean body laid in transparently — the darks are the ground, not paint
- Knife work for the wave face and the rock shelf
- Titanium White loaded heavily and pulled with the knife for the crest and foam; this is the only opaque passage
- Fan brush for the palm fronds and the foreground bushes
- Liner brush for the two trunks and the frond spines
- Spray flicked and stippled at the base of the break

No Liquid Clear is used. The transparency is in how thin the oils are carried over dry black, not in a clear medium underneath them.

Signature technique: **thin transparent oils glazed over dry black gesso, with opaque white reserved for the wave crest**

## 7. Narrative Layer

"The tide comes in hard under a bruised sky, and for one held moment the sun burns straight through the back of a collapsing wave. Two palms lean out over the rocks, taking the wind. The whole scene is built out of darkness — every glow in it had to be earned back from a black canvas."

## 8. Initial Canvas Treatment

- **Black gesso** applied to the canvas and dried completely
- No Liquid White anywhere in the painting
- No Liquid Clear — the glazing is done with thinned oil directly on the dry black
- No contact paper, no masking
- Thin, transparent applications everywhere except the foam and crest, which are loaded thick

The black ground is the painting's value structure. The sunset glow reads as light because it is the only thing on the canvas that is not black.
