# Season 4, Episode 5 — "Evening Seascape" (1985)

---

## 1. Composition
- Dramatic ocean waves dominating center and right portions
- Rocky outcroppings flanking left and right edges
- Dark scattered rocks across foreground beach
- Stormy evening sky with swirling cloud formations
- Pure seascape composition with no vegetation or structures
Compositional archetype: **Turbulent coastal drama with bilateral rock framing**

## 2. Palette
- Prussian Blue and Midnight Black for deep sky and water shadows
- Phthalo Blue and Phthalo Green in translucent wave bodies
- Titanium White for wave crests, foam, and spray
- Van Dyke Brown and Burnt Umber for rocky formations
- Indian Yellow and Yellow Ochre for shallow water highlights
- Limited warm accents against dominant cool tones

Palette identity: **Nocturnal oceanic blues with dramatic white contrasts**

## 3. Mood & Atmosphere
- Powerful and turbulent energy
- Evening darkness creating mysterious depth
- Wild, untamed coastal forces
- Cool-dominated temperature suggesting night approach
- Dramatic tension between water and stone

Lighting: **Ambient evening glow with selective wave illumination**

## 4. Structural Layout
- **Left:** Rocky outcropping extending into surf
- **Right:** Large crashing wave against dark rock formation
- **Center:** Multiple wave breaks with white foam patterns
- **Foreground:** Sandy beach with scattered dark stones
- **Background:** Storm clouds and distant wave action

Depth style: **Layered wave progression from distant sky to immediate shore**

---

## 5. Motion
- Waves crashing diagonally from background toward foreground
- Right-to-left compositional flow in primary wave structure
- Foam and spray suggesting explosive water-rock collision
- Swirling cloud movement echoing ocean turbulence
- Dynamic energy concentrated in center-right wave break

---

## 6. Technique
- Fan brush for delicate wave foam and spray effects
- Palette knife for sharp rocky edges and highlights
- Wet-on-wet blending for translucent wave bodies
- Push-pull technique creating wave dimensional structure
- Pure titanium white loaded heavily for bright foam contrast
- Dark-to-light layering building wave luminosity
- Circular strokes for cloud formations

Signature technique: **Dramatic foam highlighting against dark water base**

---

## 7. Narrative Layer
"An evening storm reveals the eternal contest between ocean and shore, where powerful waves hurl themselves against ancient rocks in endless rhythm. The fading light captures a single moment of this timeless dance, freezing the spray and foam in their violent beauty. Nature's raw power dominates this coastal sanctuary, reminding us of forces beyond human scale."

---

## 8. Initial Canvas Treatment

- **Black gesso** brushed over the canvas and allowed to **dry completely** before any oil is applied. This is a dry acrylic ground, not a wet one: the oils above it cannot be pulled back into it, and every light value in the painting has to be added rather than lifted.
- No Liquid Clear and no Liquid White base — the oils are carried thin directly over the ground.
- No contact paper and no masking.

The black ground carries the value structure of the whole painting. Read the light in this entry as light recovered from darkness, not as shadow laid over white.
