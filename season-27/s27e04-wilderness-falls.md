# Season 27, Episode 4 — "Wilderness Falls" (1993)

## 1. Composition

- Snow-capped mountain filling the upper left, its lavender face rising out of a warm haze
- Turquoise sky with soft white cloud across the top, warming to yellow-green and pink where it meets the mountain base
- A single tall **conifer** standing clear of the treeline on the right, silhouetted against the sky
- Dense bright deciduous foliage massed along the right edge and the lower left corner
- Mist-filled foothills stepping down from the mountain toward the valley floor
- A **small waterfall** dropping out of the misted foothills into a **stream** that runs forward through the lower centre-left and opens into a **pond** at the bottom of the canvas
- No structures, no figures

A mountain wilderness in which falling water carries the eye down from the peak to the viewer's feet.

Composition archetype: **Mountain vista with falling water**

> **Reference note.** The catalogue still for `painting_index 350` is a mid-episode frame:
> the mountain, sky and mist are complete, but the frame is cropped above the finished
> foreground, so the falls, stream and pond that the episode is named for sit largely
> below its lower edge. Only the pale grey-blue channel descending between the foothills
> at lower centre-left is visible in it. The water features here are taken from the
> episode's ground-truth tags (`Waterfall (Small)`, `Stream`, `Lake/Pond`), not inferred
> from the still.

## 2. Palette

- **Liquid Black** as the ground, thinned and worked into the canvas before any colour
- **Liquid Clear** over it, giving a wet transparent surface without a white base
- **Titanium White** for the snowfield, the clouds, the falls and the water highlights
- **Phthalo Blue** and **Prussian Blue** for the turquoise sky and the cool mountain shadow
- **Alizarin Crimson** mixed into white for the lavender-pink mountain face and the warm haze
- **Cadmium Yellow** and **Indian Yellow** carrying the yellow-green glow at the mountain base
- **Sap Green**, **Phthalo Green** and **Yellow Ochre** for the bright deciduous masses
- **Midnight Black** for the conifer structure and the deepest foliage
- **Van Dyke Brown** and **Dark Sienna** for trunks, branches and the rock ledges the falls run over
- **Bright Red** in small quantity, warming the foliage darks

Because the ground is black, the darks in the foliage and the conifer are largely ground showing through rather than mixed pigment.

Palette identity: **turquoise-and-lavender mountain palette glowing off a black ground**

## 3. Mood & Atmosphere

- Serene and expansive, but with real movement in the water
- Luminous early light, mist still lying in the foothills
- Warm haze against a cold peak
- Sound implied: the only noisy thing in an otherwise silent painting is the falls

Lighting: **soft backlit morning glow** with warm haze under a cold, lit peak.

## 4. Structural Layout

- **Left:** Mountain shoulder descending into misted foothills and bright low foliage
- **Right:** Tall conifer and a dense wall of green deciduous growth
- **Centre:** The mountain peak above, the water course below
- **Foreground:** Pond and stream bank, foliage edges
- **Midground:** Small falls dropping from the mist, feeding the stream
- **Background:** Lavender peak, turquoise sky, cloud

Depth style: **layered atmospheric planes linked by a falling water course**

## 5. Motion

- Water falling from the mist and running forward toward the viewer
- Mist rising off the foothills, moving against the water
- Slow cloud drift across the top of the canvas
- Vertical pull up to the peak balanced by the downward pull of the falls

Motion profile: **downward water flow against rising mist**

## 6. Technique

- **Liquid Black** worked thin into the canvas as the ground
- **Liquid Clear** laid over it so the oils stay workable without lightening the base
- Sky blended criss-cross over the clear; the transparency lets the black darken the value at the edges
- Knife work for the mountain face, the snowline and the rock ledges
- Mist pulled horizontally with a clean dry brush, lifting rather than adding
- Fan brush for the conifer and for the bright deciduous masses, pushed upward
- Falls pulled downward with the knife edge in short broken strokes, then softened at the base
- Stream and pond laid in with horizontal strokes, reflections pulled straight down and cut across
- Liner brush for fine branches and grass at the water's edge

Signature technique: **transparent oils over a Liquid Black ground, with knife-pulled falling water**

## 7. Narrative Layer

"Morning in high country. The peak has the first light on it while the valley is still holding mist, and somewhere in that mist a small fall is running, sending a stream down through the trees to the pond at your feet. The mountain is the reason you look up; the water is the reason you stay."

## 8. Initial Canvas Treatment

- **Liquid Black** applied thin and worked into the canvas as the ground
- **Liquid Clear** applied over the Liquid Black to give a wet, transparent working surface
- No Liquid White anywhere
- No black gesso — this is a wet black ground, not a dried acrylic one, so the oils above it stay in contact with it and can be pulled back into it
- No contact paper, no masking
- Thin transparent work in the sky and mist; loaded paint reserved for snow, falls and foliage highlights

The Liquid Black is what keeps the foliage and conifer darks deep without muddying them, and the Liquid Clear over it is what lets the sky stay luminous on top of a black base.
