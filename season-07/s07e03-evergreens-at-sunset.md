# Season 7, Episode 3 — "Evergreens at Sunset" (1986)

## 1. Composition
This painting showcases a dramatic sunset vista framed by silhouetted evergreen trees. The composition is anchored by two prominent foreground trees—one on the left and one on the right—that create a natural frame for the scene. Multiple layers of coniferous trees recede into the distance, establishing strong vertical elements throughout. A misty valley or fog bank occupies the middle ground, suggesting either ground fog or low-lying clouds. The sky dominates the upper half with sweeping horizontal cloud formations that catch the sunset's glow. Small grassy elements punctuate the snowy foreground. The overall archetype is a **sunset vista with framing trees**, emphasizing depth through atmospheric perspective and silhouetted layering.

## 2. Palette
The palette is split into two contrasting zones: warm brilliance and cool shadows. **Golden orange** and **bright yellow** dominate the sunset sky, creating intense luminosity at the horizon. **Deep blue** and **dark purple** form the cloud masses above, while **near-black** (mixed from blues and browns) creates the silhouetted trees and foreground elements. **Slate blue** and **misty gray** describe the fog layer in the middle ground. **White** highlights snow patches and mist.

Palette identity: **High-contrast sunset with warm/cool split—golden orange sky against deep blue-black silhouettes.**

## 3. Mood & Atmosphere
The mood is both dramatic and peaceful, capturing the fleeting magic of sunset's golden hour. The intense warmth of the glowing sky contrasts with the cool, mysterious silhouettes, creating visual tension that reads as awe-inspiring rather than unsettling. There's a sense of wilderness solitude and natural grandeur—the viewer stands among ancient evergreens witnessing nature's daily spectacle. The misty middle ground adds ethereal mystery, suggesting depth and distance while softening the transition between land and sky. The emotional resonance is contemplative wonder tinged with the poignant beauty of day's end.

Lighting: **Backlit sunset with silhouette effect.**

## 4. Structural Layout
**Left:** A large, gnarled evergreen with distinctive horizontal branching dominates the left foreground, its trunk and branches creating dark linear elements against the bright sky. **Right:** A taller, more traditionally shaped evergreen anchors the right side, its triangular silhouette perfectly framed against the sunset. **Center:** Multiple layers of smaller evergreens recede into the misty distance, creating a layered progression. The brightest portion of the sunset glows in the center background. **Foreground:** Snow-covered ground with small grassy tufts and evergreen saplings, rendered primarily in dark values. **Midground:** A band of atmospheric fog or low clouds creates separation and depth, rendered in soft blue-grays. **Background:** The dramatic sunset sky with horizontal cloud striations catching the golden light.

Depth style: **Strong atmospheric perspective with layered silhouettes and fog separation.**

## 5. Motion
The primary motion is the implied westward drift of clouds across the sunset, emphasized by horizontal brushstrokes in the sky. The misty fog bank suggests slow, rolling movement through the valley below. The gnarled branches of the foreground trees create implied windswept motion, their twisted forms suggesting years of prevailing winds. Compositional flow lines move from the dark foreground trees upward and inward toward the glowing center of the sunset, drawing the eye to the brightest point on the horizon. The vertical thrust of the evergreens contrasts with the horizontal sweep of clouds, creating dynamic tension.

Motion profile: **Horizontal cloud drift with vertical tree thrust; windswept foreground elements.**

## 6. Technique
This painting relies heavily on **silhouette technique**, where foreground and midground trees are rendered in dark values against a bright sky—achieved by painting the bright sky first, then adding dark tree shapes over it. The sunset sky employs **wet-on-wet blending** with horizontal crisscross strokes to merge yellows, oranges, and blues. **Fan brush technique** creates the delicate evergreen foliage in the distance, while **liner brush work** defines individual branches and twigs on the foreground trees. The misty fog uses **soft blending** and possibly a **soft brush or paper towel technique** to create atmospheric haze. The gnarled tree branches showcase **fine detail work** with a liner brush, creating organic, twisted forms. Snow on the ground is applied with the **palette knife** for texture.

Signature technique: **Dramatic silhouette layering with backlit sunset.**

## 7. Narrative Layer
"Stand among the ancient evergreens as day surrenders to night, the sky ablaze with amber and gold while the forest falls into shadow. The mist rises from the hidden valley below, a soft veil between earth and heaven. This is the threshold moment—neither day nor night, but the sacred in-between where light and darkness dance their eternal ballet. Here, in the cathedral of trees, witness the quiet majesty that unfolds when the world pauses to watch the sun's farewell."

## 8. Initial Canvas Treatment

This episode uses a preparation that appears nowhere else in the index, and none of it is
visible in the finished painting.

- **Acrylic yellow ground.** The whole canvas is painted yellow with an ordinary acrylic
  paint and allowed to dry completely. This is not Liquid White, not gesso, and not a
  transparent tone — it is an opaque dried colour ground.
- **Shaped tape mask.** A circle of duct tape is stuck to the dry yellow ground where the
  sun will be. Everything is painted over and around it; the tape is lifted at the end and
  the bare yellow ground underneath *is* the sun.
- **Liquid Black** is then brushed on very sparingly over the dry yellow, laid in as loose
  horizontal bands rather than as an even coat — the sky's dark cloud structure is put in
  before any oil colour at all.
- **Alizarin Crimson** worked into those bands next, then the rest of the sunset.
- A very smooth canvas matters more than usual here, because the brush has to slide over
  the dry acrylic.
- No Liquid White, no Liquid Clear, no gesso.

The sun in this painting was never painted. It is a hole in the picture where the ground
shows through.

> **Recovered from the episode narration**, not from the image:
> *"I've got a canvas, as you can probably see, that's painted yellow. And this is just an
> acrylic yellow paint, and I've covered the entire canvas with it. And right here is just —
> this is a piece of duct tape I found here in the studio… but I've just made a circle right
> there. This canvas is dry today. And onto this, we're going to begin putting a little bit
> of the liquid black color, and I'm going to make a beautiful little sunset out of it."*
